##### Hanbo Zhan

# Algorithmic Smoothness and the End of Subjectivity

<div class="body-text">

Technological capitalism claims that eliminating waiting, satisfying needs in advance, and channelling desire smoothly toward immediate return is a great enhancement of human agency. This claim presupposes that friction is, by default, an obstacle to the subject, so that to eliminate friction is to liberate the subject.
This paper argues the opposite: friction is in fact a necessary condition for the constitution of the subject, and the technological apparatus that systematically eliminates it structurally destroys the very ground on which the subject takes shape. If the genesis of subjectivity depends on specific formal conditions, then a technology aimed at eliminating those conditions necessarily triggers the structural collapse of subjectivity.

**I. The Subject Is Sustained by Friction**

By friction I mean the ineliminable structural resistance between the subject and the objects of its desire. Lacan distinguishes need from desire: need has a concrete object and subsides once satisfied, whereas desire points to no concrete object and circulates perpetually around a structural lack that cannot be filled (Lacan, 2006). As Žižek observes, this lack within the symbolic structure is the subject itself (Žižek, 2009). Once the lack is filled, desire vanishes, and the subject with it. What sustains the subject is precisely the fact that the lack cannot be filled. This is the first source of friction.

The second source is temporal. In Cinema 1, Deleuze locates subjectivity in the interval between stimulus and response: inanimate matter moves the instant it is acted upon, whereas a living centre hesitates, and it is precisely this non-zero delay that opens the space of interiority and choice (Deleuze, 1986). Once the interval falls to zero, response degenerates into reflex. What sustains the subject here is that the response cannot be given immediately.

The lack and the delay are both a gap: the distance between what the subject desires and what it already possesses. Friction is not this gap itself but the pressure the gap exerts; it is the result of the gap, and the gap in itself is not a force. If I am hungry with nothing to eat, the missing meal is the gap; the hunger that drives me to look for food is the pressure it exerts, and that driving force is the resistance. The absence of food does not, in itself, push me; it is the felt state of hunger that sets me in motion. No intentionality is implied here: nothing resists the subject on purpose. The resistance is merely the tension of a state that can never be closed — and it is precisely by pressing against this tension that the subject comes to feel that it exists.

Although these two sources belong respectively to a theory of desire and a philosophy of time, they point to the same structural feature: the subject depends on a resistance that cannot be reached directly. Lacan’s lack is spatial unreachability; Deleuze’s interval is temporal unreachability; the two are dimensions of one and the same resistance. This paper uses the word friction to name this feature, and concludes: the subject is sustained by friction, and to eliminate friction is to strip away the necessary condition for sustaining the subject.

**II. Prestress and Load**

Friction is heterogeneous, comprising two structurally distinct components. The first is a permanent, background resistance, which I name alterity: the way the world and others remain beyond the subject’s will, holding to an irreducible opacity — Lacan’s big Other (Lacan, 2006). In the structural analogy, it corresponds to prestress: an internal tension that exists prior to any external load and holds the structure in form.

The second is a contingent resistance, arising in one concrete situation after another, which I name the frustration of desire: each instance of wanting and not obtaining, in which desire is continually reignited and structured. It corresponds to load: the external forces the structure bears, one after another.

This translation holds because both fields confront the same problem: how a thing maintains its own unity in the absence of a self-sufficient point of support. Lacan removed the Cartesian cogito as that point;thereafter, unity is maintained by a relational structure organized by lack, in which no privileged entity grounds the whole (Lacan, 2006; Descartes, 2008). Tensegrity confronts the very same problem when it refuses the foundation, and gives the same answer: distributed relational tension. The correspondence rests on this formal isomorphism.

**III. Tensegrity**

On the basis of this legitimate translation, the paper argues, by construction, that the structure able to sustain the subject must be, and can only be, tensegrity. A tensegrity structure consists of two kinds of member: a continuous network of cables in tension, and compression struts that do not touch one another and float within the tension network. Its core mechanical feature is prestress: even with no external load, the cable network is already taut and the struts already compressed, and the structure’s form and stiffness are shaped entirely by this internal opposition that precedes any external force (Fuller, 1982; Calladine, 1978).

These members find exact counterparts. The struts are the subject’s desires: each presses outward, and no desire touches another directly. The cables are friction, the tension that binds these outward pressures into one whole form. The empty spans between the struts — the non-contact the cables hold taut — are the unreachable itself, and it has two dimensions at once: a spatial gap (Lacan’s lack) and a temporal gap (Deleuze’s interval). A single tensioned band already carries both properties: it has a length in space and a duration over which it stays taut. Space and time are therefore not two different members but two projections of the one resistance the cables embody.

From this, three constraints follow. One: load-bearing relies on overall tension, with no foundation — Lacan’s subject can bear force only through a tension network spread across the whole. Two: the struts must float and never touch — physical non-contact is the structural equivalent of Deleuze’s temporal interval. Three: once tension is withdrawn, the structure disintegrates — losing the tension that maintains lack, it degenerates into loose parts. Tensegrity alone satisfies all three at once. Calladine’s theorem consolidates this: an underconstrained tensegrity acquires stiffness in a state of self-stress, and once the self-stress is withdrawn the structure collapses (Calladine, 1978). The mode of failure matters: a wall stripped of its foundation crashes, whereas a tensegrity stripped of its tension does not crash but slackens — the cables go loose, the struts drift, and the form quietly sags out of existence.

**IV. The Subject as a Living Space Pried Open by Tension**

What truly holds this structure up is the relational field sustained by tension; the members are what that field holds in place. Mapped onto the subject, the subject itself is this space pried open by force. The void here is a living void — tautened and sustained by the tension around it, holding its form only through that tension’s continuous action. It is wholly distinct from a dead, silent emptiness: the living void has form and stiffness, deforming under force and rebounding when force is released. Once the tension disappears, this living space closes.

**V. The Smoothness of the Algorithm and the Weightlessness of the Subject**

The subject is a tensegrity supported jointly by the two components of friction; the smoothness of the algorithm is, at the structural level, the erasure of both at once. Norman defines the ideal interface as one that disappears completely (Norman, 1998) — a philosophy that erases the interval on which subjectivity depends, and one enforced rigorously at the lowest layer of the data pipeline.

On one hand, algorithms erase prestress. Feature extraction and data scraping reduce the body and contingent events in dimensionality into structured feature vectors (Bradski, 2000; Lugaresi et al., 2019), and the alterity that cannot be captured parametrically is discarded as noise. The environment is remade into a compliant mirror, and unpredictable otherness dies away with it. Read together with Han’s account of the disappearance of the other (Han, 2018), the permanent background tension that holds the subject up is withdrawn.

On the other hand, algorithms erase load. The loss function minimizes the error between predicted and actual values (Goodfellow, Bengio and Courville, 2016), while prediction pre-empts conflict before it occurs. As Zuboff argues, surveillance capitalism extracts profit by eliminating the uncertainty of behaviour in advance (Zuboff, 2023). The external forces the subject would have borne are intercepted before frustration can occur: desire is filled in before it can be denied.

When both components are withdrawn, the tension field slackens and the subject falls into weightlessness: the internal opposing force that maintains its form disappears. And because pain was the felt result of friction, eliminating friction eliminates pain along with it, and the dismantling is undergone, from inside, as comfort. Opposition is precisely one of the elements by which a structure takes shape.

**VI. Conclusion**

The persistence of the subject relies on alterity as prestress and on the frustration of desire as load. Within this framework, the smoothness pursued by contemporary algorithmic systems is a systematic dismantling of the structure of the subject: recommendation flattens the world and withdraws the prestress, while prediction fills desire in before it can be frustrated and cancels the load. This dual absence dissolves the subject’s tension field and closes its living space, pried open by tension, in a frictionless environment. In seeking to abolish all resistance and delay, technological capitalism also destroys the very ground on which the subject takes shape. And the end does not announce itself: there is no crash, only a slackening that is felt, in so far as it is felt at all, as ease.
